Collin Lucken

Hastings Postdoctoral Scholar in AI & Humanity

Bowdoin College

I am a philosopher of science investigating the epistemic and ethical dimensions of artificial intelligence engineering, in two directions. The first rehabilitates the maker’s knowledge tradition to explain how making minds advances cognitive science: the instruments, robots, and experiments researchers build are discoveries in their own right. The second unpacks what AI engineers owe everyone else. I work with two kinds of tool at once, the careful argument of philosophy and the computational methods of artificial intelligence, so AI for me is both something I use and something I study. As the Hastings Postdoctoral Scholar in AI and Humanity at Bowdoin College, I also help students and faculty decide how, and whether, to bring AI into their own teaching and research.

Philosophy of Science & AI

Artificial Intelligence, Scientific Progress, and the Maker’s Knowledge Tradition

The maker’s knowledge tradition claims we know best what we’ve made. I argue this follows from making's material severity: making is discriminatingly unforgiving, that is, it reveals to the maker where they lack relevant knowledge when they do. This reorientation explains how making plays such a crucial role in science.

Engineering Ethics & Public Engagement

Engineering, Artificial Intelligence, and the Common Good

What do AI engineers owe everyone else? I argue that engineering is a morally significant professional identity whose regulative ideal is the development of central human capabilities, that democratizing AI means restructuring who participates in shaping it, and that these obligations follow the engineer all the way into frontier AI labs.
